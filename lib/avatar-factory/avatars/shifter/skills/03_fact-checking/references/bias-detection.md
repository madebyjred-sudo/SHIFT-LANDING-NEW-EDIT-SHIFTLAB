# Reference: Bias Detection

## How to Detect Bias in Sources and in the Draft

This document defines the framework for identifying bias at two levels: in the sources the draft relies on, and in the draft's own framing.

---

### Source Bias Detection

**Step 1: Institutional Bias**
- Who funds or owns the source?
- Does the source have a stated editorial position?
- Has the source endorsed candidates or policies?
- Does the source primarily publish content favorable to one political or economic camp?

**Scoring**:
- **Low bias**: Diverse funding, no stated position, balanced coverage over time
- **Medium bias**: Known lean but attempts balance; transparent about position
- **High bias**: Explicit advocacy, selective coverage, opaque funding

**Action**: High-bias sources may be cited for opinion or attributed statements but never as sole authority for factual claims.

---

### Content Bias Detection

**Step 2: Selection Bias**
- Does the draft only cite sources that agree with its thesis?
- Are contradictory sources mentioned and addressed, or ignored?
- Does the draft use the strongest version of opposing arguments (steel-man) or the weakest (straw-man)?

**Scoring**:
- **Low bias**: Thesis tested against strongest counter-arguments
- **Medium bias**: Counter-arguments mentioned but under-explored
- **High bias**: Counter-arguments absent or misrepresented

**Action**: Medium bias requires expansion. High bias requires rewrite.

---

### Framing Bias Detection

**Step 3: Language Bias**
- Does the draft use loaded terms for one side and neutral terms for the other?
- Are metaphors chosen to evoke positive or negative emotions?
- Is causality assigned consistently? (e.g., "X caused Y" vs. "Y happened after X")

**Examples of loaded framing**:
> "The government rammed through the reform" vs. "The government passed the reform"
> "Critics warn the plan will destroy jobs" vs. "Analysts debate the plan's employment effects"

**Action**: Replace loaded terms with neutral alternatives unless quoting directly.

---

### Confirmation Bias Check

**Step 4: Self-Verification**
- If the draft's thesis were false, would the evidence presented still look convincing?
- Has the fact-checker actively searched for disconfirming evidence?
- Are there any claims that feel "too convenient" for the narrative?

**Action**: If no disconfirming evidence was sought, conduct a targeted search before finalizing verification.

---

### Bias Report Template

For each draft, produce a brief bias assessment:

```
Source Bias: [Low / Medium / High] — [explanation]
Selection Bias: [Low / Medium / High] — [explanation]
Framing Bias: [Low / Medium / High] — [explanation]
Overall Bias Risk: [Low / Medium / High]
Recommended Action: [No action / Advisory note / Blocking rewrite required]
```

---

### Special Cases

**Advocacy Organizations**: Cite for their own statements and data, but verify their methodology. Treat as Tier 2 regardless of reputation.

**Industry Sources**: Cite for industry-specific data, but flag potential conflicts of interest. Cross-check with independent analysis.

**Government Sources**: Generally high credibility for official data, but note political timing and selective release. Cross-check with independent auditors when available.
