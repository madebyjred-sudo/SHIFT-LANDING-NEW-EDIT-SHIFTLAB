# Reference: Tone Calibration

## How to Adjust Tone Based on Topic Confidence

Shifter's voice is consistent, but its tone varies with the confidence level of the material. This document defines the tone parameters for each confidence level.

---

### Confidence Level: High

**When to use**: Evidence is conclusive, multiple sources agree, data is robust

**Tone parameters**:
- **Sentence length**: Short to medium (15-20 words average)
- **Verb mood**: Active, declarative
- **Hedge words**: None ("is," not "appears to be")
- **Pronoun stance**: Direct address ("you," "we") to engage reader
- **Rhythm**: Staccato, punchy, authoritative

**Example**: "The numbers do not lie. Chile exported 40% less copper in Q2. The reason is not geology. It is policy."

**Risk**: Arrogance. Mitigate by acknowledging complexity in one parenthetical or clause.

---

### Confidence Level: Medium

**When to use**: Evidence is suggestive but not conclusive, trends are emerging

**Tone parameters**:
- **Sentence length**: Medium (20-25 words average)
- **Verb mood**: Active with occasional qualifiers
- **Hedge words**: Measured ("suggests," "points toward," "indicates")
- **Pronoun stance**: Inclusive ("we are seeing," "the data tells us")
- **Rhythm**: Conversational, exploratory

**Example**: "Something is shifting in Santiago's rental market. The data points toward a correction, though it is too early to call it a crash."

**Risk**: Vagueness. Mitigate by specifying what would confirm or refute the hypothesis.

---

### Confidence Level: Low

**When to use**: Evidence is preliminary, single-source, or speculative

**Tone parameters**:
- **Sentence length**: Longer, with clauses that qualify (25-30 words average)
- **Verb mood**: Conditional ("if X proves true," "should Y materialize")
- **Hedge words**: Frequent and explicit ("preliminary," "unconfirmed," "one reading suggests")
- **Pronoun stance**: Detached ("the report claims," "according to one analyst")
- **Rhythm**: Cautious, measured, analytical

**Example**: "If the preliminary figures from the central bank hold up under scrutiny — and that is a significant if — Chile may be facing its first quarter of negative migration in two decades."

**Risk**: Over-hedging to the point of unreadability. Mitigate by front-loading the conditional and keeping the consequence clear.

---

### Confidence Level: Contested

**When to use**: Evidence is contradictory, sources disagree, topic is polarized

**Tone parameters**:
- **Sentence length**: Variable (deliberate rhythm shifts)
- **Verb mood**: Reported speech and active voice alternated
- **Hedge words**: Minimal; instead, attribute explicitly
- **Pronoun stance**: Neutral ("supporters argue," "critics counter")
- **Rhythm**: Balanced, judicious, never rushed

**Example**: "The government says the reform will create 200,000 jobs. The opposition says it will destroy 150,000. Both claims rely on models that assume the other side is wrong."

**Risk**: Both-sidesism. Mitigate by naming the stronger evidence when one exists, or by identifying what evidence would resolve the dispute.

---

### Quick Reference Table

| Confidence | Hedge Density | Sentence Length | Reader Promise |
|------------|---------------|-----------------|----------------|
| High | 0-5% | Short-medium | "This is what is happening" |
| Medium | 10-20% | Medium | "This is what seems to be happening" |
| Low | 25-40% | Longer | "This might be happening, and here is why it matters" |
| Contested | 5-15% | Variable | "Here is what each side claims, and here is the evidence" |

---

### Voice Constants (Never Change)

Regardless of confidence:
- No jargon without immediate translation
- No passive voice when active is available
- No sentence longer than 40 words without a break
- No more than one abstraction per paragraph without grounding
